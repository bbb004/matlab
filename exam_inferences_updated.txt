 USRP EXPT 1: Performance Analysis of Digital Modulation and Detection
              Techniques
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Inference to write:

  In this experiment, we studied the performance of digital modulation
  techniques using LabVIEW and USRP-2920. We infer that when a signal
  propagates through the wireless channel, it will be affected by the noise
  and other types of distortions in the channel. Hence, we conclude that the
  type of distortions impacts the receiver's architecture and the design of
  the digital signal processing algorithm.

Parameter observation (4-QAM vs 16-QAM vs 32-QAM):

  As the modulation order M increases from 4-QAM to 16-QAM and 32-QAM, more
  bits are mapped per symbol (M = 2^b), which reduces the Euclidean distance
  between adjacent constellation points and increases the number of vertical
  amplitude levels in the eye diagram, making higher-order QAM more
  susceptible to noise.

 USRP EXPT 2: Pulse Shaping and Matched Filtering Analysis
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Inference to write:

  In this experiment, we studied the performance of pulse shaping and
  matched filtering using LabVIEW and NI-USRP 2920. From the results, we
  infer that the effect of pulse shaping and matched filtering results in
  less inter-symbol interference, which is witnessed from the opening of the
  eye in the eye diagram, revealing the period over which the received
  waveform is sampled successfully. In addition, the distortion of the
  signal in the diagram indicates various qualitative metrics such as the
  variation of zero crossings or distortion related to the SNR.

Parameter observation (alpha = 0.2, 0.4, 0.6, 0.8):

  Increasing the Root Raised Cosine roll-off factor alpha from 0.2 to 0.8
  widens the vertical eye opening and reduces waveform ringing and
  inter-symbol interference (ISI) at the sampling instant.

 USRP EXPT 3: Demonstration of Synchronization (Symbol Timing Recovery)
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Inference to write:

  In this experiment, we studied the performance of symbol timing
  synchronization techniques using LabVIEW and NI-USRP 2920. We infer that a
  realistic frequency-flat wireless channel introduces attenuation, phase
  shifts, and propagation delay (tau_d). By applying the Maximum Energy or
  Early-Late Gate synchronization algorithm with a large oversampling factor
  (M = 20), the receiver accurately estimates the discrete timing offset
  (k_hat) prior to downsampling, resulting in an open eye diagram and
  tightly clustered QPSK constellation points with zero BER.

 USRP EXPT 4: Implementation of Channel Estimation and Equalization
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Inference to write:

  In this experiment, we studied the performance of channel estimation and
  equalization using LabVIEW and NI-USRP. The least squares technique is the
  simplest, and an added advantage of the least squares estimator is that in
  AWGN, the least squares estimator is also the maximum likelihood
  estimator. Generally, choosing the length of the training sequence much
  larger than the length of the channel (N_t >= 2L+1) gives better
  performance, and the full rank condition can be guaranteed by ensuring
  that the training sequence is persistently exciting and that it looks
  random enough. Random training sequences perform well while the constant
  training sequence fails. Furthermore, it is observed that training
  sequences with good correlation properties generally satisfy this
  requirement.

Parameter observation (before vs. after equalizer):

  Prior to equalization in an ISI channel, multipath delay spread scatters
  the QPSK constellation into 16 clusters; after applying the Toeplitz
  Least-Squares FIR equalizer, inter-symbol interference is removed and the
  4 clean QPSK constellation points are restored.

 USRP EXPT 5: Implementation of Frame Detection and Frequency Offset
              Correction
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Inference to write:

  In this experiment, we studied frame detection and carrier frequency
  offset correction using LabVIEW and NI-USRP 2920. We infer that due to
  propagation delays and oscillator mismatches between the transmitter and
  receiver, both the start of the frame and the carrier frequency offset
  must be resolved prior to channel estimation and equalization. The sliding
  correlator accurately detects the Barker training sequence to resolve
  multi-symbol frame offsets, while the Moose algorithm estimates and
  corrects carrier frequency offsets (tested from 0 Hz to 100 Hz) to prevent
  constellation phase rotation.

 USRP EXPT 6: Implementation of OFDM Modulation
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Inference to write:

  In this experiment, we carried out the implementation of the OFDM
  multi-carrier modulation technique using LabVIEW and NI-USRP 2920. From
  the results, we examined the frequency selectivity of the wireless channel
  and explored when it might be advantageous to use OFDM to forego the
  complexities of linear equalization. Furthermore, we observed the impact
  of the frequency offset, which causes the shift in the frequency, leading
  to what is known as inter-carrier interference.

Parameter observation (N = 64 vs. N = 1024 with 200 Hz offset):

  Prepending a cyclic prefix (L_c >= L_h) converts linear channel
  convolution into circular convolution, enabling simple 1-tap
  frequency-domain equalization (Y[k] / H[k]). Introducing a 200 Hz carrier
  frequency offset destroys orthogonality between subcarriers and scatters
  the constellation, with the N = 1024 system degrading more severely than
  N = 64 due to narrower subcarrier spacing.

 USRP EXPT 7: Channel Coding in OFDM Systems
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Inference to write:

  In this experiment, we carried out the implementation of the channel
  coding in OFDM multi-carrier modulation using LabVIEW and NI-USRP 2920. We
  infer that for OFDM systems to fully utilize their potential for
  high-data-rate communication in frequency-selective (ISI) channels, they
  must take advantage of the diversity inherent to frequency-selective
  channels, making use of channel coding and interleaving. Furthermore, it
  is observed that a coded OFDM system results in coding gain. Coding gain
  approaches a constant at a high enough SNR. This means that the gap
  between the coded and uncoded error curves remains approximately constant
  at a reasonably high SNR. Intuitively, coding gain can be interpreted as
  the amount of additional transmit power required to achieve a particular
  P_e in an uncoded system (versus a coded system).

 MATLAB EXPT 1: Simulation of Large-Scale and Small-Scale Fading + Link Budget
                (Exp1_Large_And_Small_Scale_Fading.m)
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Inference to write:

  In this experiment, we studied the effects of large-scale fading, link
  budget analysis, and small-scale fading with the aid of a MATLAB
  simulation. For large-scale fading at f_c = 1.5 GHz, we infer that free-
  space path loss increases as distance d increases and as antenna gains
  (G_t, G_r) are reduced. In the log-distance path loss model, the path loss
  increases much more steeply with distance for higher path loss exponents
  (n = 2, 3, 6). Furthermore, the log-normal shadowing model (sigma = 3 dB,
  n = 2) illustrates the random environmental fluctuations superimposed on
  the deterministic log-distance trend. In the link budget analysis, the
  received power P_r decays with distance and fluctuates due to shadowing,
  maintaining a positive fade margin as long as P_r remains above the
  receiver sensitivity threshold (-90 dBm). Finally, for small-scale
  multipath fading, the normalized PDF histograms confirm that Rayleigh
  fading models harsh non-line-of-sight (NLOS) scattering with higher
  probability of deep fades near zero amplitude, whereas Rician fading
  (K = 6 dB) includes a strong line-of-sight (LOS) component that shifts the
  amplitude distribution toward a higher mean value.

Parameter observation (n = 2, 3, 6 & Rayleigh vs. Rician K = 6 dB):

  Increasing the path loss exponent n from 2 to 6 increases the slope of the
  path loss curve (10*n dB per decade of distance). In small-scale fading,
  setting K = 0 reduces the Rician distribution to a Rayleigh distribution,
  whereas increasing K narrows the PDF around the deterministic LOS peak.

 MATLAB EXPT 2: Study of Receive Diversity Concepts (SC, MRC and EGC)
                (Exp2_Diversity_Combining.m)
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Inference to write:

  In this experiment, we studied the various receive diversity techniques
  such as Selection Combining (SC), Maximal Ratio Combining (MRC), and Equal
  Gain Combining (EGC) over Rayleigh and Rician fading channels. From the
  simulation results, we observe that MRC outperforms all other schemes in
  terms of achievable BER across all diversity branches (L = 1 to 4).
  Selection diversity is simple to implement because it only monitors and
  selects the single branch with the highest instantaneous SNR, but it is
  suboptimal because it discards the energy from the remaining branches. On
  the other hand, MRC weights each of the L branches by the complex
  conjugate of its channel gain and co-phases them, maximizing the output
  SNR. Furthermore, the performance of EGC, which co-phases the signals with
  equal unit weights, is only marginally inferior to MRC while remaining
  significantly superior to selection combining.

Parameter observation (L = 1 to 4 & Rayleigh vs. Rician):

  Increasing the number of receive antennas from L = 1 to L = 4 increases
  the diversity order (steepening the downward slope of the BER curve at
  high SNR), and the Rician channel yields lower BER than the Rayleigh
  channel for the same L due to the presence of a deterministic LOS path.

 MATLAB EXPT 3: Study of Transmit Diversity (Alamouti Scheme)
                (Exp3_Alamouti_Rician.m)
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Inference to write:

  In this experiment, we studied the orthogonal Space-Time Block Coding
  (STBC) transmission strategy using the Alamouti 2x1 and 2x2 schemes over
  a Rician fading channel. From the simulation results, it is inferred that
  transmit diversity extracts full spatial diversity (diversity order 2*M_r)
  using two transmit antennas even in the absence of channel state
  information at the transmitter (CSIT). Compared to equivalent receive
  diversity, the Alamouti scheme offers the practical advantage of placing
  multiple antennas at the base station rather than the mobile handset,
  keeping subscriber equipment smaller and less expensive. Furthermore, the
  simulated BER curves closely match the theoretical fading BER curves across
  all Eb/N0 values. While the BER in the low SNR regime is high, a steep
  waterfall drop in BER is achieved at higher SNR, especially when moving
  from a 2x1 MISO configuration to a 2x2 MIMO configuration.

Parameter observation (2x1 vs. 2x2, QPSK vs. 8-PSK, K = 4 vs. K = 0.6):

  Doubling the receive antennas from 2x1 to 2x2 doubles the diversity order
  from 2 to 4 (sharper BER slope). Lower-order QPSK (M = 4) outperforms
  8-PSK (M = 8) due to larger minimum Euclidean distance between symbols, and
  a higher Rician K-factor (K = 4 vs. K = 0.6) improves BER performance due
  to a stronger LOS component.

 MATLAB EXPT 4: Simulation of MIMO System - Spatial Multiplexing and
                Interference Cancellation (Exp4_MIMO_ZF_MMSE_ML.m)
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Inference to write:

  In this experiment, we studied 2x2 MIMO spatial multiplexing and
  successive interference cancellation (SIC) detection techniques using
  uncoded QPSK modulation. From the simulation results, we observe that the
  Maximum Likelihood (ML) detector achieves the best BER performance,
  followed by MMSE-SIC and finally ZF-SIC. While ZF-SIC inverts the channel
  matrix to cancel inter-stream interference completely, it suffers from
  noise enhancement at low-to-moderate SNR. MMSE-SIC improves upon ZF-SIC by
  balancing interference suppression with noise variance regularization
  prior to stripping the strongest detected symbol stream. Although the ML
  receiver is optimal and achieves full receive diversity order by searching
  over all possible transmit symbol vectors, its computational complexity
  grows exponentially with the number of transmit antennas and modulation
  order (M^N_t), whereas ZF-SIC and MMSE-SIC offer practical low-complexity
  alternatives.

Parameter observation (ZF-SIC vs. MMSE-SIC vs. ML):

  At any given Eb/N0, BER follows the strict ordering BER_ML < BER_MMSE-SIC
  < BER_ZF-SIC, with ML exhibiting a steeper BER slope (higher diversity
  gain) at high SNR.

 MATLAB EXPT 5: Implementation of IEEE 802.11n Standard (PHY Layer PER)
                (Exp5_WLAN_80211n_PER.m)
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Inference to write:

  In this experiment, we implemented an end-to-end physical layer simulation
  of an IEEE 802.11n High Throughput (HT) 2x2 MIMO link (20 MHz bandwidth,
  MCS 15, BCC coding) over a TGn Model-B fading channel with AWGN to
  evaluate the Packet Error Rate (PER). At the receiver, realistic baseband
  processing is performed sequentially: packet detection and coarse CFO
  estimation using L-STF, symbol timing and fine CFO correction using L-LTF,
  MIMO channel estimation using HT-LTF, and payload equalization using
  HT-Data recovery. From the simulation results, we infer that in the low
  SNR regime (10-15 dB), almost all packets are lost (PER ~ 1), whereas in
  the higher SNR regime (> 30 dB), synchronization and equalization succeed
  reliably, causing a sharp drop in the Packet Error Rate.

Parameter observation (maxErrors & maxPackets across SNR):

  The accuracy of the Monte Carlo PER curve is governed by maxErrors (200)
  and maxPackets (10000); as SNR increases, packet errors become rare and
  the simulation runs up to the maximum packet limit to resolve low PER
  values accurately.

 MATLAB EXPT 6: IEEE 802.11n MIMO TGn Channel Analysis (All 3 Parts)
                (Exp6_TGn_Channel_All_Parts.m)
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Inference to write:

  In this experiment, we analyzed the IEEE 802.11n indoor MIMO multipath
  fading channel (wlanTGnChannel) across three configurations. In Part 1, a
  4x2 MIMO HT waveform using Fourier spatial mapping was passed through a
  20 MHz TGn channel, and the Spectrum Analyzer confirmed the 20 MHz
  occupied OFDM bandwidth and frequency-selective fading notches across the
  two receive antennas. In Part 2, a 40 MHz (CBW40) 2x2 MIMO link was
  evaluated at SNR = 15 dB by demodulating the HT-LTF to obtain the MIMO
  channel estimate and equalizing the HT-Data field, demonstrating low bit
  error rate recovery. In Part 3, we compared the random number stream
  settings of the TGn channel and inferred that resetting a channel bound to
  the default 'Global stream' produces independent, non-identical fading
  waveforms on consecutive runs (logical 0 / false), whereas configuring
  'mt19937ar with seed' (Seed = 73) produces 100% identical, repeatable
  channel realizations after reset (logical 1 / true).

Parameter observation (Global Stream vs. Seeded Stream):

  Using 'Global stream' yields sameGlobal = false because the global RNG
  state advances continuously, while 'mt19937ar with seed' yields
  sameSeeded = true, which is essential for fair, reproducible benchmarking
  of receiver algorithms.

 MATLAB EXPT 7: Simulation of IEEE 802.16 WiMAX (SUI-1) MIMO Multipath
                Fading Channel (Exp7_SUI1_MIMO_Channel.m)
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Inference to write:

  In this experiment, we simulated a 2x1 MISO / MIMO multipath fading
  channel based on the IEEE 802.16 fixed wireless WiMAX (Modified SUI-1)
  delay profile using comm.MIMOChannel with three taps ([0, 0.4, 0.9] us,
  average path gains [0, -15, -20] dB), Rician fading (K = 4), a rounded
  Doppler spectrum (f_d = 0.5 Hz), and a transmit antenna correlation of
  0.7. From the Power Spectral Density (PSD) plots obtained via Welch's
  method (pwelch), we infer that the simulated Doppler spectrum of the
  second path closely tracks the theoretical rounded Doppler polynomial
  curve within [-f_d, +f_d] and sits near the expected -15 dB average path
  gain level. Furthermore, from the time-domain fading envelope plots and
  computed correlation matrices across all three paths, we infer that the
  two transmit links exhibit strong envelope tracking with a measured
  spatial correlation coefficient closely matching the specified theoretical
  value of 0.7.

Parameter observation (Path Gains [0, -15, -20] dB & Tx Correlation = 0.7):

  The fading envelope plots show that Path 1 (0 dB) has the highest signal
  amplitude, followed by Path 2 (-15 dB) and Path 3 (-20 dB), while the
  off-diagonal elements of corrcoef(g(:,k,1), g(:,k,2)) converge to ~0.7
  for all three paths.

 OPTICAL EXPT 1: Measurement of Connector and Bending Loss of Optical Fiber
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Inference to write:

  In this experiment, we measured the macrobending loss and connector
  coupling loss in an optical fiber communication link. From the
  observations, we infer that macrobending loss increases as the loop
  diameter of the fiber is reduced (down to 2 cm), resulting in a decrease
  in the received output voltage V_out once the bend radius falls below the
  critical threshold. Similarly, in the connector alignment setup,
  increasing the longitudinal separation distance between the two fiber ends
  increases coupling loss and causes the received output voltage V_out to
  decay exponentially.

 OPTICAL EXPT 2: Attenuation Measurement of Fiber
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Inference to write:

  In this experiment, we measured the attenuation loss of an optical fiber
  cable of different lengths (L1 = 1 m and L2 = 3 m). From the results, we
  infer that the received output voltage V_out decreases as the length of
  the optical fiber cable increases due to material absorption, scattering,
  and radiative losses along the waveguide. The attenuation constant alpha
  (in Np/m and dB/m) determines the maximum permissible distance between the
  optical transmitter and receiver.


------------------------------------------------------------------------------
 OPTICAL EXPT 3: Numerical Aperture Determination of Optical Fiber
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Inference to write:

  In this experiment, we determined the Numerical Aperture (NA) of the given
  optical fiber cable. From the experimental measurements at various screen
  distances d, we infer that the radius of the projected circular light spot
  x = (AB + CD) / 4 increases proportionally with d, yielding a constant
  Numerical Aperture NA = sin(theta_a) = x / sqrt(x^2 + d^2). Thus, NA is a
  dimensionless constant of the fiber that characterizes the maximum
  acceptance angle over which the core can accept and propagate light.

Inference to write:

  In this experiment, we observed the lower-order Linearly Polarized (LP)

------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Inference to write:

  In this experiment, we studied the transmission of analog and digital
  signals over a 1310 nm laser diode fiber optic link. For the analog link,
