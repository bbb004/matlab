 USRP EXPT 1: Performance Analysis of Digital Modulation and Detection
              Techniques (M-QAM)
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

THEORY:
  Digital modulation maps discrete binary bit sequences onto the amplitude
  and phase of a high-frequency analog carrier signal. In M-ary Quadrature
  Amplitude Modulation (M-QAM), two orthogonal carriers—In-phase (I) and
  Quadrature (Q)—are amplitude-modulated simultaneously. Each symbol
  represents b = log2(M) bits.

  As the modulation order M increases (from 4-QAM to 16-QAM and 32-QAM),
  spectral efficiency (bits/s/Hz) increases. However, for a fixed average
  transmit power, packing more constellation points into the I-Q plane
  reduces the minimum Euclidean distance (d_min) between adjacent points.
  Consequently, higher-order QAM requires a higher Signal-to-Noise Ratio
  (SNR) to maintain a low Bit Error Rate (BER) and displays more vertical
  amplitude levels in the eye diagram.


------------------------------------------------------------------------------
 USRP EXPT 2: Pulse Shaping and Matched Filtering Analysis
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

THEORY:
  Transmitting rectangular pulses requires infinite bandwidth. When passed
  through a band-limited wireless channel, rectangular pulses spread in the
  time domain, causing adjacent symbols to overlap—a distortion known as
  Inter-Symbol Interference (ISI).

  To satisfy the Nyquist zero-ISI criterion while conserving bandwidth, the
  overall Raised Cosine filter response is split equally between a Root
  Raised Cosine (RRC) pulse-shaping filter at the transmitter and a matching
  RRC filter (Matched Filter) at the receiver. The cascaded response ensures
  that the pulse tails cross zero at exact integer multiples of the symbol
  period T_s, maximizing the SNR at the sampling instant. The roll-off
  factor alpha (0 <= alpha <= 1) controls the excess bandwidth. Increasing
  alpha widens the vertical eye opening and reduces time-domain sidelobe
  ringing.

 USRP EXPT 3: Demonstration of Synchronization (Symbol Timing Recovery)
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

THEORY:
  When a signal travels over a wireless channel, propagation delay (tau_d)
  causes an unknown timing offset between the transmitter symbol clock and
  the receiver sampling clock. Sampling away from the optimal peak of the
  matched filter output introduces severe ISI and degrades the SNR.

  To recover symbol timing, the received baseband signal is oversampled by a
  factor M (e.g., M = 20 samples per symbol). The synchronization block
  evaluates the energy across all M polyphase sampling branches over an
  observation window and selects the optimal sampling phase k_hat that
  maximizes the average output energy (corresponding to the widest vertical
  eye opening). Downsampling the signal at index k_hat restores clean
  constellation clusters.

 USRP EXPT 4: Implementation of Channel Estimation and Equalization
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

THEORY:
  A multipath frequency-selective channel acts as a Finite Impulse Response
  (FIR) filter that smears transmitted symbols across multiple symbol
  durations, scattering a 4-point QPSK constellation into multiple clusters.

  To estimate the unknown channel impulse response h, the transmitter sends
  a known training sequence t of length N_t. By arranging the training
  symbols into a Toeplitz convolution matrix T, the receiver computes the
  Least-Squares (LS) channel estimate. The training sequence must be
  persistently exciting (random-like with sharp autocorrelation) and satisfy
  N_t >= 2L + 1 so that T^H * T is full rank and invertible. Using the same
  LS formulation, an FIR equalizer w is designed to invert the channel
  distortion and restore the original QPSK constellation.


------------------------------------------------------------------------------
 USRP EXPT 5: Implementation of Frame Detection and Frequency Offset
              Correction
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

THEORY:
  In asynchronous packet transmission, the receiver must determine the exact
  starting sample of an incoming frame and compensate for Carrier Frequency
  Offset (CFO) caused by local oscillator mismatches between the transmitter
  and receiver. Uncompensated CFO causes a continuous phase rotation
  (2*pi*delta_f*t), spinning the received constellation.

  Frame detection is achieved using a sliding correlator matched to a known
  Barker sequence (which possesses sharp peak autocorrelation and minimal
  sidelobes). Once the frame start is found, the Moose algorithm estimates
  the CFO using two identical, consecutive training halves of length N_t. By
  computing the phase angle of the complex correlation between the first and
  second training halves, the frequency offset delta_f_hat is estimated and
  removed via complex derotation.


------------------------------------------------------------------------------
 USRP EXPT 6: Implementation of OFDM Modulation
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

THEORY:
  Orthogonal Frequency Division Multiplexing (OFDM) converts a single
  high-rate frequency-selective wideband channel into N parallel, orthogonal,
  flat-fading narrowband subcarriers using an N-point Inverse Fast Fourier
  Transform (IFFT) at the transmitter and an FFT at the receiver.

  Prepending a Cyclic Prefix (CP) of length L_c >= L_h (where L_h is the
  channel delay spread) absorbs inter-symbol interference (ISI) and converts
  the linear convolution of the multipath channel into circular convolution.
  This allows simple 1-tap frequency-domain equalization (dividing each
  subcarrier by its complex channel gain H[k]). However, OFDM is highly
  sensitive to Carrier Frequency Offset (CFO), which destroys subcarrier
  orthogonality and introduces Inter-Carrier Interference (ICI). Larger FFT
  sizes (e.g., N = 1024 vs. N = 64) have narrower subcarrier spacing
  (delta_f = B / N), making them more vulnerable to a fixed CFO.


------------------------------------------------------------------------------
 USRP EXPT 7: Channel Coding in OFDM Systems
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

THEORY:
  In an uncoded OFDM system operating over a frequency-selective multipath
  channel, subcarriers that fall into deep spectral nulls experience severe
  SNR degradation, dominating the overall Bit Error Rate (BER).

  To exploit the frequency diversity inherent in the multipath channel,
  Forward Error Correction (FEC) coding (such as convolutional or block
  coding) is combined with bit interleaving across subcarriers. The encoder
  introduces structured redundancy (code rate R_c = k / n), while the
  interleaver separates adjacent coded bits onto distant subcarriers so that
  a deep fade bursts errors across independent codewords rather than
  overwhelming a single codeword. At high SNR, the coded OFDM system achieves
  a horizontal SNR advantage over the uncoded system known as Coding Gain.

 MATLAB EXPT 1: Simulation of Large-Scale and Small-Scale Fading + Link Budget
                (Exp1_Large_And_Small_Scale_Fading.m)
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

THEORY:
  Wireless propagation models are classified into large-scale and small-scale
  fading:
  1. Large-Scale Propagation Models predict average signal power attenuation
     over large transmitter-receiver separation distances (d):
     - Free-Space Path Loss (Friis Model) assumes unobstructed line-of-sight
       (LOS) propagation where power decays inversely with d^2.
     - Log-Distance Path Loss generalizes attenuation to practical
       environments using the path-loss exponent n (n = 2 for free space,
       n = 3 to 6 for urban/obstructed buildings).
     - Log-Normal Shadowing accounts for random surrounding clutter by
       adding a zero-mean Gaussian random variable X_sigma (in dB) with
       standard deviation sigma to the log-distance model.
  2. Link Budget calculates the received power P_r (in dBm) after accounting
     for transmit power, antenna gains, system losses, and path loss. The
     difference between P_r and the receiver sensitivity threshold is the
     Fade Margin.
  3. Small-Scale Fading describes rapid amplitude fluctuations over short
     distances due to constructive and destructive multipath interference:
     - Rayleigh Fading models non-line-of-sight (NLOS) scattering using the
       magnitude of a zero-mean complex Gaussian process.
     - Rician Fading models a dominant stationary LOS component combined
       with scattered multipath components, parameterized by the Rician
       K-factor (ratio of LOS power to scattered power).


------------------------------------------------------------------------------
 MATLAB EXPT 2: Study of Receive Diversity Concepts (SC, MRC and EGC)
                (Exp2_Diversity_Combining.m)
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

THEORY:
  Receive spatial diversity mitigates multipath deep fades by equipping the
  receiver with L independent antennas. The probability that all L branches
  simultaneously experience a deep fade decays as (1 / SNR)^L. The received
  signal on branch i is r_i = h_i*s + n_i. Three linear combining techniques
  are used:
  1. Selection Combining (SC): Monitors the instantaneous SNR on all L
     branches and selects only the single branch with the maximum channel
     magnitude |h_i|^2. It requires the simplest hardware but ignores energy
     from the remaining L - 1 branches.
  2. Equal Gain Combining (EGC): Co-phases all L branches by multiplying each
     branch by exp(-j*angle(h_i)) and sums them with equal unit amplitude
     weights.
  3. Maximal Ratio Combining (MRC): Weights each branch by the complex
     conjugate of its channel gain (w_i = h_i*), simultaneously co-phasing
     the signals and weighting them proportionally to their strength. MRC
     maximizes the post-combining output SNR (gamma_MRC = sum gamma_i) and
     achieves the lowest BER (MRC > EGC > SC).

 MATLAB EXPT 3: Study of Transmit Diversity (Alamouti Scheme)
                (Exp3_Alamouti_Rician.m)
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

THEORY:
  The Alamouti scheme is an orthogonal Space-Time Block Code (STBC) that
  achieves full transmit diversity order (2*N_r) using N_t = 2 transmit
  antennas and N_r receive antennas without requiring Channel State
  Information at the Transmitter (CSIT).

  Two modulated symbols (s_1, s_2) are transmitted across two consecutive
  time slots, assuming the fading channel gains (h_1, h_2) remain constant
  over the two-slot block:
  - In Time Slot 1: Antenna 1 transmits s_1, Antenna 2 transmits s_2.
  - In Time Slot 2: Antenna 1 transmits -s_2*, Antenna 2 transmits s_1*.
  Total transmit power per slot is normalized by dividing each symbol by
  sqrt(2). Because the code matrix is orthogonal, the receiver decouples s_1
  and s_2 using simple linear combining without matrix inversion.

 MATLAB EXPT 4: Simulation of MIMO System - Spatial Multiplexing and
                Interference Cancellation (Exp4_MIMO_ZF_MMSE_ML.m)
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

THEORY:
  In a V-BLAST Spatial Multiplexing MIMO system (N_t = 2, N_r = 2),
  independent symbol streams are transmitted simultaneously from each
  antenna to multiply data throughput by N_t. The received vector is
  y = H*s + n, where inter-stream interference occurs between antennas:
  1. Zero-Forcing with Successive Interference Cancellation (ZF-SIC):
     Computes the Moore-Penrose pseudoinverse W_ZF = (H^H * H)^(-1) * H^H to
     null interference completely, but suffers from noise enhancement when H
     is ill-conditioned.
  2. Minimum Mean Square Error with SIC (MMSE-SIC): Regularizes the matrix
     inversion with noise variance N_0 (W_MMSE = (H^H*H + N_0*I)^(-1)*H^H),
     balancing interference suppression and noise amplification.
     In both SIC schemes, the stream with the smallest post-detection noise
     norm is detected first, re-modulated, and subtracted from y (y_rest =
     y - h_1*s_hat_1) before detecting the remaining stream.
  3. Maximum Likelihood (ML) Detector: Performs an exhaustive search over
     all M^(N_t) candidate symbol vectors C, selecting the vector that
     minimizes Euclidean distance ||y - H*s||^2. ML achieves optimal BER and
     full diversity order, followed by MMSE-SIC and ZF-SIC.

 MATLAB EXPT 5: Implementation of IEEE 802.11n Standard (PHY Layer PER)
                (Exp5_WLAN_80211n_PER.m)
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

THEORY:
  The IEEE 802.11n standard combines MIMO spatial multiplexing with OFDM to
  achieve high throughput in indoor wireless local area networks (WLANs).
  Each transmitted HT packet (PPDU) contains structured preamble fields
  followed by the data payload (PSDU):
  1. L-STF (Legacy Short Training Field): Used at the receiver for coarse
     packet detection (wlanPacketDetect) and coarse Carrier Frequency Offset
     estimation (wlanCoarseCFOEstimate).
  2. L-LTF (Legacy Long Training Field): Used for fine symbol timing
     synchronization (wlanSymbolTimingEstimate) and fine CFO correction
     (wlanFineCFOEstimate).
  3. HT-LTF (High-Throughput Long Training Field): Orthogonal training
     symbols transmitted across all spatial streams to estimate the MIMO
     subcarrier channel matrix H (wlanHTLTFChannelEstimate).
  4. HT-Data: Carries the Binary Convolutional Coded (BCC) payload, which is
     equalized and decoded using H and the noise variance (wlanHTDataRecover).
  A packet error is recorded whenever one or more bits in the recovered PSDU
  differ from the transmitted PSDU, or when packet synchronization fails.

 MATLAB EXPT 6: IEEE 802.11n MIMO TGn Channel Analysis (All 3 Parts)
                (Exp6_TGn_Channel_All_Parts.m)
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

THEORY:
  1. Spatial Mapping (Part 1): When the number of transmit antennas (N_t = 4)
     exceeds the number of space-time streams (N_sts = 2), a spatial mapping
     matrix Q (such as a Fourier orthogonal matrix) maps the streams across
     all transmit antennas. Passing the waveform through wlanTGnChannel
     introduces frequency-selective multipath fading visible on a Spectrum
     Analyzer.
  2. MIMO Channel Estimation & Recovery (Part 2): Over a 40 MHz bandwidth
     (CBW40, sampling rate 40 MHz), demodulating the received HT-LTF field
     yields the complex channel matrix estimate H_hat, enabling zero-forcing
     or MMSE equalization of the HT-Data symbols.
  3. Random Stream Repeatability (Part 3): Stochastic channel models rely on
     pseudorandom number generators (PRNGs). When RandomStream is set to
     'Global stream', calling reset() still draws new random values from
     MATLAB's advancing global state, yielding independent channel fades
     (isequal = false). Setting RandomStream to 'mt19937ar with seed' with a
     fixed seed (e.g., Seed = 73) re-initializes the local Mersenne Twister
     generator upon reset(), guaranteeing identical channel realizations
     (isequal = true) for reproducible receiver benchmarking.

 MATLAB EXPT 7: Simulation of IEEE 802.16 WiMAX (SUI-1) MIMO Multipath
                Fading Channel (Exp7_SUI1_MIMO_Channel.m)
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

THEORY:
  The Stanford University Interim (SUI-1) channel model characterizes fixed
  broadband wireless access (IEEE 802.16 WiMAX) in flat terrain with light
  tree density. It is modeled as a tapped-delay-line filter with three
  discrete multipath taps (delays [0, 0.4, 0.9] us and average path gains
  [0, -15, -20] dB) following a Rician distribution (K = 4).

  Unlike mobile cellular channels that exhibit a U-shaped Jakes/Clarke
  Doppler spectrum, fixed wireless channels experience Doppler spread
  primarily from wind-blown foliage, modeled by a 'Rounded' polynomial
  Doppler spectrum bounded within [-f_d, +f_d] (f_d = 0.5 Hz). Furthermore,
  insufficient physical spacing between transmit antennas introduces spatial
  correlation (specified via a 2x2 TransmitCorrelationMatrix with off-
  diagonal coefficient rho = 0.7), causing the fading envelopes of the two
  transmit links to fluctuate in a correlated manner.

 OPTICAL EXPT 1: Measurement of Connector and Bending Loss of Optical Fiber
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

THEORY:
  Light propagates through an optical fiber core via Total Internal
  Reflection (TIR) as long as the angle of incidence at the core-cladding
  interface exceeds the critical angle theta_c = arcsin(n_2 / n_1).

  1. Macrobending Loss: When an optical fiber is bent into a tight loop of
     small diameter, the local angle of incidence of guided rays at the
     outer bend wall falls below the critical angle theta_c. Consequently,
     evanescent field energy radiates out into the cladding, causing optical
     power (and detected output voltage V_out) to drop sharply below a
     critical bend radius.
  2. Connector Coupling Loss: When two fiber ends are separated by a
     longitudinal air gap, the cone of light emerging from the transmitting
     fiber diverges according to its Numerical Aperture. As separation
     distance increases, a smaller fraction of the emitted light cone falls
     within the core area of the receiving fiber, causing an exponential
     decay in coupled power.


------------------------------------------------------------------------------
 OPTICAL EXPT 2: Attenuation Measurement of Fiber
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

THEORY:
  As an optical signal propagates along an optical fiber, its optical power
  decays exponentially with distance according to Beer-Lambert's Law. This
  intrinsic signal attenuation is caused by three primary physical
  mechanisms:
  1. Material Absorption: Intrinsic absorption by silica glass molecules and
     extrinsic absorption by trace metal or OH^- water-ion impurities.
  2. Rayleigh Scattering: Microscopic fluctuations in glass density and
     refractive index (smaller than the optical wavelength lambda) scatter
     light in all directions proportional to 1 / lambda^4.
  3. Waveguide Imperfections: Microbending and core-cladding interface
     irregularities.
  By measuring the photodetector output voltages V_1 and V_2 for two fiber
  lengths L_1 and L_2 under identical launch conditions, connector coupling
  losses cancel out and the attenuation coefficient alpha (in dB/m or Np/m)
  is obtained.


------------------------------------------------------------------------------
 OPTICAL EXPT 3: Numerical Aperture Determination of Optical Fiber
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

THEORY:
  The Numerical Aperture (NA) of an optical fiber is a dimensionless number
  that measures the light-gathering ability of the fiber core. It is defined
  as the sine of the maximum acceptance half-angle (theta_a) within which an
  incident ray entering from air into the fiber core will be trapped and
  guided via Total Internal Reflection (TIR).

  Rays entering at an angle greater than theta_a strike the core-cladding
  boundary at less than the critical angle and refract out into the cladding.
  Experimentally, light exiting the fiber projects a circular spot of radius
  x onto a screen placed at a distance d from the fiber tip. By divergence
  symmetry, the exit cone half-angle equals the acceptance half-angle
  theta_a.


------------------------------------------------------------------------------
 OPTICAL EXPT 4: Study of Cylindrical Mode Step Index / Graded Index Fiber
                 (LP Modes)
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

THEORY:
  An optical fiber is a cylindrical dielectric waveguide. Light propagating
  along the core forms discrete electromagnetic field distributions (standing
  wave patterns across the cross-section) that satisfy Maxwell's equations
  and boundary conditions, known as Guided Modes. Under the weakly guiding
  approximation (n_1 ~= n_2), these modes are designated as Linearly
  Polarized (LP_{lm}) modes, where l is the azimuthal index (half the number
  of bright lobes in a ring) and m is the radial index (number of concentric
  rings).

  The number of guided modes is governed by a dimensionless parameter called
  the Normalized Frequency or V-number. If V < 2.405, the fiber supports
  only the fundamental LP_01 mode (Single-Mode Fiber). For V > 2.405, the
  fiber operates as a Multimode Fiber supporting higher-order modes such as
  LP_11, LP_21, and LP_02, which can be selectively excited by adjusting the
  laser launch angle.

 OPTICAL EXPT 5: Study of Fiber Optic Analog and Digital Link
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

THEORY:
  A complete optical fiber communication link consists of an electrical-to-
  optical (E/O) transmitter (Laser Diode or LED operating at 1310 nm), an
  optical fiber waveguide, and an optical-to-electrical (O/E) receiver
  (PIN or Avalanche Photodiode coupled with a transimpedance amplifier).

  1. Analog Link: An input sinusoidal signal modulates the drive current of
     the laser diode around a DC bias point (Intensity Modulation). At the
     receiver, the photodiode converts the optical intensity variations back
     into an electrical sine wave. Sweeping the input frequency allows
     measurement of the frequency response and the 3 dB cutoff bandwidth
     (f_3dB), where voltage gain drops by 3 dB (or to 1/sqrt(2) = 0.707 of
     its mid-band value).
  2. Digital Link: A TTL square wave switches the optical source between ON
     (logic 1) and OFF (logic 0) states (On-Off Keying). The receiver
     comparator reconstructs the digital pulse train, preserving the pulse
     width (T_ON), space width (T_OFF), and percentage duty cycle.
